# Build something real. In seven phases.

The Part IB group design project is your first taste of a professional software
project: a real client, a real team you didn't choose, and a deliberately hard
brief. This is everything you need to keep on top of it — the phases, the
deadlines, and the requirements that earn your four ticks.

## Twenty-three briefs, twenty-three real clients

From a nanomedicine puzzle game to a SystemVerilog compiler, an accessibility
tool for blind learners to a thermal-imaging survey of leaky houses. Every brief
comes with an external client — and they are deliberately hard.

## The requirements that actually decide your ticks

Most of the course is yours to design. These six rules are not — they run through
every phase, and getting one wrong is the usual way a good project loses marks.

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   **60 hours per member**

    ---

    The project plan must work to an overall budget of no more than 60 hours per
    person. Projects are expected to take 30–60 hours, spread over seven weeks.

-   **Four compulsory client meetings**

    ---

    Meetings in weeks 1, 2, 4 and 6 of Lent term. Attendance is compulsory —
    failure to attend will jeopardise one or more of your ticks.

-   **Copy everything to the project email**

    ---

    Copies of all deliverables must be sent to the project organisers, as these
    are used for assessment. The team name must appear in the subject line.

-   **PDF, 12 noon the day before**

    ---

    Every formal document is a PDF, emailed to the client before 12 noon on the
    day preceding the meeting, so the client has time to review it.

-   **Everyone contributes technically**

    ---

    Every member must make a substantial technical contribution — test harnesses,
    scripts, data conversion, interfaces, demos or core code.

-   **Tue & Thu, 11:00–13:00**

    ---

    The lecture timetable reserves these slots on every Tuesday and Thursday of
    Lent term, giving explicit time for groups to meet and co-ordinate.

</div>

!!! danger "Everything must be copied to the group project email address"

    "It is essential that copies of all deliverables are sent to the project
    organizers, as these copies will be used for assessment." If the organisers
    don't have it, it wasn't submitted — no matter what your client received.

## Seven phases, from brief to awards ceremony

Each phase has its own kind of activity, scheduled across different weeks and days
as decided in your own group's project plan. This is the shape of the course; the
full detail of each phase, with the tasks somebody has to own, is on
**[The Seven Phases](team-phases.md)**.

| Phase | When | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I** · Briefing and project selection | Michaelmas term | Design briefs are presented, and you record the projects you would most like to work on. |
| **II** · Kickoff and risk analysis | Week 1 | Teams are announced, a contact person is elected, and the first client meeting sets the requirements. |
| **III** · Requirements analysis | Week 2 | Turn open-ended requirements into a formal functional specification and a costed project plan. |
| **IV** · Implementation | Weeks 2–6 | Write, test and integrate the code, reporting progress at meetings 3 and 4. |
| **V** · Wrapup | Weeks 6–7 | Finalise and document the code, film a four-minute video, and write the final group report. |
| **VI** · Presentation and awards | Week 8 | Videos go online, the whole department votes, and the term closes with a public exhibition and prizes. |
| **VII** · Reflection and review | By 5pm, last Wednesday of Lent | An individual personal report reflecting on the experience, your contribution, and your team. |


## Four meetings, four documents

There are four formal review meetings with your client — in person or virtual —
in weeks 1, 2, 4 and 6 of Lent term. It is your group's responsibility to arrange
each one within the required week, and to email the preceding PDF before 12 noon
the day before (copied to the group project address, team name in the subject).

| Meeting | When | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meeting 1** | Week 1 | Clarify the client's requirements. Meet as soon as you can. A risk assessment (≤50 words) follows within 24 hours. |
| **Meeting 2** | Week 2 | Feedback on the functional specification and project plan. The draft reaches the client at least 24 hours beforehand. |
| **Meeting 3** | Week 4 | Progress and feedback. Progress Report 1 is sent as a PDF at least 24 hours before. |
| **Meeting 4** | Week 6 | Progress and feedback. Progress Report 2 is sent as a PDF at least 24 hours before. |

!!! info "Reserved co-ordination time"

    The lecture timetable reserves 11:00–13:00 on every Tuesday and Thursday of
    Lent term, giving explicit time for groups to meet and co-ordinate internally.

## Four ticks, and the expectation you get all four

Performance is not assessed relative to other students. One tick recognises that
the project succeeded; three recognise what you personally achieved.

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   **The group tick**

    ---

    Awarded to all group members, recognising that the project has succeeded.

-   **Worked with your team**

    ---

    You worked effectively with the members of your team.

-   **Worked with your client**

    ---

    You maintained a professional relationship with your client.

-   **Contributed technically**

    ---

    You personally made a substantial technical contribution.

</div>

How each is awarded, plus the interim self-assessment and the personal report, is
covered on **[Guidance](team-guidance.md)**.
